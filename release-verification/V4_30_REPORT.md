`versionCode = 61`, `versionName = "4.30"`. Base: `PaperScrape_v4_29.zip`, SHA-256
`b59bab19…c61a6e2b`, 1 765 files, verified by `sha256sum` before extraction.

Legend: **[M]** measured, with the command that produced it · **[O]** observed in the code · **[D]**

---

## 0. The four things to read first

1. **A person is no longer shipped once per colour.** It is fixed art plus one weight mask per
   colourable region — skin, head, shirt, trousers — recomposed as `fixed + Σ (mask × colour)` at
   the blit. **168 tone copies left the set** — twelve of them the winter window recolours no draw
   path could reach, which are the four shapes that were retired rather than converted — and 195
   layer files joined it. The point is not the file count: it is that **a colour axis stopped multiplying
   the set**. Hair, shirt and trousers arrived in this release at the cost of the masks; the same
   three shipped the old way would have been twenty-seven copies of every person.
2. **The mask is summed, not laid over, and that is correctness rather than taste.** Two source-over
   layers split a pixel's coverage, `a + b(1-a)` is not linear, and `SpriteDetailLevel` halves each
   layer separately before upload — so they stop recomposing and a halo appears at every edge, 63
   levels of coverage out of 255 on a real device frame. Proved by breaking it: switching the masks
   to source-over fails `people-skyscraper` and `people-single` at once (§7.2).
3. **Four regions cost zero draw calls, counted on the device.** One `glDrawArrays` per frame on
   three crowded themes, and no standalone texture across a twelve-theme sweep. The plan predicted
   twenty-four extra draws a frame; it was wrong for a reason v4.29 created (§4).
4. **Two premises this release was given turned out to be false, and both were measured rather than
   argued.** The investigation's own §1 says a mask carries three regions and its §A.2 says one —
   §A.2 is right (`BACKLOG_v4_30.md` item 94). And the crossing counter is **not** off screen by
   construction: it is in view 52.8 % of the time (item 95, §5.2).

## 2. The decomposition

### 2.1 A region is a piece, not a colour **[M]**

The generator draws the figure, so it knows which polygon is hair. The piece name was already
written into every `cut` call as its wobble seed; v4.30 carries it onto the `Part` and
`build_people_concepts.region_of` maps it to one of four regions.

**Deciding by colour does not work, and the number is specific:** the man's hair, his shoes, his
eyes and his ground shadow are all painted `#2B2A33`, and of the 3 167 pixels of that colour on
`person_man_summer_walk0`, **868 are not hair**. Worse, `#2B2A33` *is* `SHADE_TOWARDS`, the colour
every shadow in this artwork moves toward — so for the man the shade family that a colour-based
decomposition needs is degenerate and there is nothing to fit.

The masks are then rendered through the **same drawing code** (`emit_region_svg`), with each piece
painted its own weight in grey instead of its paint. Geometry, z-order and under-papers are the same
objects, so the render's own anti-aliasing resolves a pixel shared between two pieces exactly the
way the shipped render resolves it, and the alpha that comes back is the figure's.

### 2.2 The weight, and the one rounding in it **[O]**

A shadow in this artwork is `(1-t)·paint + t·DARK` — both `shade()` and the Rilievo under-paper —
so a piece's own fill says how far toward `DARK` it was moved, and `1 - t` is the share of the pixel
that follows the region's colour. Recovered from the two colours rather than declared at the call
site, and a piece whose fill is not on that segment is a hard error: that is what would catch a
piece tagged into a region it is not drawn in.

A mask ships as **white with the weight in its alpha**. `BitmapFactory` premultiplies on load, so
the texture carries `rgb · alpha / 255 = W` through **one** quantisation instead of two. The weights
are **floored**, not rounded: the drawing is `Σ W_r·B_r + F` with `F` a coverage times a colour and
therefore non-negative, so taking each weight down to the nearest level leaves the fixed layer a sum
of non-negative terms by construction. Rounding to nearest overshoots by half a level per region and
drives the fixed layer below zero at an anti-aliased edge, where it is then clipped and shows.

### 2.3 It adds up to the drawing **[M]**

`tools/generate_people_layers.py` refuses to write a set that recomposes worse than two levels; the
worst across all 48 shapes is **1.184 levels of one channel**, and it is the rasteriser's own
rounding rather than the decomposition's — resvg hands back a fully covered patch of the man's skin
as 219 where his paint is 220, on 61 pixels of one frame.

`PeopleLayerAssetTest` re-derives the same claim from the **shipped files alone**, and does it
without being told the palette: for each region it recovers the colour from pixels that region owns
outright, then requires that colour to close the sum over every pixel of the sprite. That is
strictly stronger than being handed the right answer — a mask claiming a pixel it does not own, or a
fixed layer keeping a share it should have given up, fails it. Worst error over the 30 shapes that
still ship a base drawing: **2 levels of one channel**, which is the tolerance and is reached by
most of them -- it is the rasteriser's own rounding plus the mask's single quantisation, and a
mis-tagged piece misses by tens rather than by two (§7.2 is the demonstration).

Shown to bite: weakening a 15×15 patch of one skin mask by 12 levels reports `person_man_summer_walk0: 11`
(§7.2).

## 3. The two ceilings, moved in opposite directions

| | v4.29 | v4.30 | limit | margin |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| texels uploaded | 17 921 692 B | **14 594 984 B** | 18 MiB → **15 MiB** | 1 133 656 B |
| bytes decoded | 33 286 896 B | **36 912 672 B** | 32 MiB → **36 MiB** | 836 064 B |

**[M]**, both from the tests that hold them, at the reference 1080×2340.

**Why they move apart.** A mask is a full canvas in the PNG and a few tenths of one in texels,
because `GlTextureCache` crops the transparent border **after** the reduction. So the GPU is charged
for the ink and the decoded ceiling for the file.

**Why the PNG is not cropped instead**, which would have paid both: `SpriteDetailLevel.reduced`
truncates. A 117-wide canvas reduced twice is 29 texels of 4.0345 authored pixels each; a 96-wide
crop of it is 24 texels of 4.0000. The two layers of one figure start together and drift apart
across the sprite — measured by the investigation at **dE 5.56** at the device's own level and
**14.38** one level up. Cropping *after* has no step to match, because the crop's texels **are** the
canvas's texels; the only thing that changes is what the bilinear tap reads just outside the ink,
and there it reads the transparent texel `GlTextureAtlas.PADDING` already puts between two entries.

**The texel ceiling went down, and that is deliberate.** The investigation framed the prize as
margin under a fixed 18 MiB. This ceiling's own convention — *set just above the measured figure, so
the pass after this one has to come here and argue too* — says otherwise, and a ceiling left 4.3 MiB
above the set is a ceiling that admits the next 4.3 MiB without anybody arguing for it. The durable
prize was never the slack: it is that a colour axis no longer multiplies the set.

**The decoded ceiling went up by 3 625 776 B**, and the argument is in its KDoc rather than here.
The short form: this limit protects the `Canvas` path, the APK and the decode peak; the GPU limit
protects what the device spends on every frame, and v4.29 established that it is the one that binds.
Shipped the old way, the three colour axes this release adds would have been ~66 MiB here and
3.4 MiB of texels — not a ceiling to raise but a road that ends.

**Cropping is not a person-only gain.** Measured by the same test, which reports its own split: the
set uploads **14 594 984 B**, of which **3 335 204 B** is people, against **18 820 592 B** if the
transparent border were not cropped after the reduction. The crop gives back **4 225 608 B**, and
most of that is not people. **[M]**

## 4. The draw count, counted rather than reasoned about

The brief asked for this number before anything else, because the arithmetic has been wrong on this
project twice. `GlDrawCallTest` reads `GlSceneTarget.drawCalls` off the final frame of three crowded
scenes, on the BV6600, with **four** regions. **[M]**

```
GlDrawCallTest: crowd-city: 1 draw(s), 3723 vertices, 168 sprite blits
              | crowd-spring: 1 draw(s), 5295 vertices, 212 sprite blits
              | crowd-winter: 1 draw(s), 6972 vertices, 228 sprite blits
```

**One `glDrawArrays` per frame.** The investigation measured the same with **one** mask; four does
not change it, and two deliberate choices are why:

- **the additive contribution travels in the sign of the vertex alpha**, not in a blend state. The
  obvious way to add is `glBlendFunc(GL_ONE, GL_ONE)`, and it would flush the batch at every layer —
  two draws per pedestrian, exactly the cost the plan feared. `step` and `abs` in the fragment
  shader cost two instructions and no state change;
- **the masks are ordinary sprites in the one atlas.** `GlAtlasOccupancyTest` sweeps the twelve
  built-in themes at full density in a single context: **270 sprite-and-level entries, 0 standalone
  textures, 516 of 2 048 atlas rows** **[M]**. A standalone texture ends the batch wherever the draw
  order crosses it, and v4.29's whole result was removing them.

So a region costs **six vertices in a batch that was already open**. It did not stop being zero.

## 5. The colours, and when they may move

### 5.1 What is dealt **[O]**

`PeopleColours` deals from *(theme, person, crossing)*:

- **skin**, three tones — the shipped ones, moved from the artwork into a constant. **Rotated, not
  re-rolled**: `PedestrianPopulation` deals a walker's tone as a stratified rank so that a street
  carries a spread however few people are on it, and a re-roll would have thrown that away for
  nothing. `PeopleColours.toneIndex` adds a per-crossing rotation, which is a bijection over the
  three: the figure moves and the deal underneath it survives;
- **head**, from the hair palette (black / brown / blond) for a bare head and the cap palette
  (red / blue / green) for a covered one. Which applies is a fact about the drawing and comes from
  `PeopleLayerTable.HEAD_WORN`, generated. **Nobody's headwear is removed to make room for hair**;
- **outfit**, five shirt-and-trouser **pairs**. Dealt as pairs because two independent lists put a
  yellow shirt over cream trousers as readily as over charcoal; every colour is one the wallpaper
  already prints.

The winter boy has no head region at all: what he wears is his own coat's hood, drawn in the coat's
paint and continuous with it, so it belongs to the shirt region and changes colour with the coat. A
summer woman and a summer girl have no trouser region: their legs are painted in their own skin, so
the skin region covers them. Both are correct and both are declared rather than inferred
(`tools/assets/tests/test_variants.py`).

Age, sex, group, lane, direction and speed are **not** re-dealt: `PedestrianPopulation.build` stays
a pure function of theme and density, and the crossing enters nothing but the colours.

### 5.2 When it may move, and the premise that failed **[M]**

The plan said the crossing counter is off screen by construction. Measured on the scene's own
geometry:

| | |
|---|---:|
| share of a crossing during which no copy of a walker is on screen | **47.2 %** |
| share of scroll positions at which the counter turns over **in view** | **52.8 %** |

A car has one body and drives off the edge; a pedestrian is **tiled**, so the wrap hands the figure
from one copy to the next without moving it, and whether that is visible depends on the scroll.

So the colour is a pure function of *(theme, person, crossing)* — the determinism the goldens need —
and the crossing **in force** is held per walker and moved only when that walker's own draw pass
reports nothing drawn. That is `PedestrianCarry.nextCarrying`'s shape, which v4.28 built for the
umbrella; the plan's own note on umbrella colours says to reuse it rather than invent it.

### 5.3 The clock, not the uptime **[O]**

`PeopleColours.clockSeconds(hour24)` — the hour that moved the sun, whether real or fixed.
`elapsedSeconds` starts at zero with the process, so counting off it would deal the same opening
hand after every restart. With a fixed hour the deal is frozen, which is the correct reading of a
frozen sun and is also what lets the goldens be goldens.

**Real randomness is refused**, and not on taste: it is visually indistinguishable — in both cases
the colour only moves off screen — and it would destroy `people-skin`, the only automatic check the
colour system has.

## 6. The children

**0.65 of an adult**, the maintainer's choice from three photographed proportions (phase 1). Not a
scaled adult: the head keeps the size it had and the height comes off the legs first and then the
torso, with the stride and arm swing shortened to match.

Measured on the regenerated artwork, alpha box on the shared canvas **[M]**:

| | man | woman | boy | girl | child / adult |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| px | 247 | 246 | 162 | 161 | **0.6552** |

Against 191 / 193 px and 0.779 before. On the 80-unit metre the size table uses that is **52.4
units**, so the declaration moved 62 → 52 and the implied real height 1.356 m → 1.138 m.

**The number lived in four places and all four moved** — `SceneSpace.PERSON_METRES_TALL`'s KDoc,
`VehiclePedestrianScaleTest.CHILD_SPRITE_UNITS_TALL` with its `0.775` and its "1.356 m",
`PrecipitationScaleTest.childMetres`, and the generator, where it is now derived from one constant
so a fifth place cannot appear.

**The busts in cars and at windows are untouched**, and phase 1 measured that they need not move:
the seated child's head was already 0.883–0.893 of an adult's and the walking child's 0.846, and the
65 % walker leaves the walker's head exactly where it was. `OccupantHeadFitTest` still holds the
seated child at 0.9 of an adult head.

**The consequence nobody asked for**, recorded rather than acted on: `PrecipitationScaleTest` held
the longest raindrop to 0.44 of *a child*, and a shorter child makes the same unchanged drop 0.51 of
one. Shrinking shipped rain as a side effect of a change to children would be an unasked-for change;
raising the fraction would be raising a tolerance to stay green. The ceiling is restated against the
adult — the figure the scene's metre is actually defined by, which did not move — at exactly the
value it has always had in those terms. `BACKLOG_v4_30.md` item 99, and whether 0.51 of a child is
too much rain is a question for a photograph.

## 7. Verification

### 7.1 The goldens, attributed before they were re-authored **[M]**

Nine Canvas goldens failed. Rather than read each against its committed file, **every** Canvas
golden was rendered on **both** builds — v4.29 extracted from its own delivery ZIP, and v4.30 — on
the same device in the same session, using a `-e dumpFrames true` switch added to `SceneGolden` for
the purpose. Two things came out of it:

**Every v4.30 pixel is inside the pavement-and-road band.** Over all 30 goldens the changed region
is `y 508..675` of an 800-row frame — the pavement, the carriageway and the building fronts, which
is where pedestrians, window occupants and car occupants are drawn. Not one pixel outside it.

**And six goldens were already carrying device drift before this release began:**

| golden | v4.29's own render vs its committed file |
|---|---:|
| `lake-dolphin-leap` | **384 px** (67 % of its 576-pixel budget) |
| `shops-closed-night` | 112 px |
| `lake-boats` | 62 px |
| `night` | 23 px |
| `traffic-day`, `traffic-day-sparse` | 14 px |

All six passed, being under the gate — but `lake-dolphin-leap` had 192 pixels of room left, so
v4.30's 399 pixels of legitimate change is what pushed it over. The cause, for the worst one, is a
bird's wing-flap: a vertical mirror switched by `sin(seconds · 9 + phase · 6.28)`, sampled at about
1800 radians, caught on the other side of a zero crossing from the frame the golden was authored
from. `BACKLOG_v4_30.md` item 98; re-authoring here resets all six.

**Re-authored twice, and the second time is worth saying.** The first pass captured a build in
which the street's tone was an independent roll per crossing. That threw away something
`PedestrianPopulation` had been doing on purpose since v4.20 — dealing tones as a **stratified
rank**, so the members of a stratum carry all three between them and a street of four people carries
a spread instead of leaving it to three independent rolls. The fix is to **rotate** the dealt tone
by the crossing rather than re-roll it: a rotation is a bijection over the three tones, so a figure
still moves every crossing and the deal underneath it survives (`PeopleColours.toneIndex`,
`PeopleColoursTest`). The goldens were re-authored again on that build, and the second change is
45–59 pixels per frame, all in rows **612..651** — the walkers' skin and nothing else.

**30 Canvas goldens re-authored. The 3 GL references are untouched**, and deliberately: they are
Adreno-authored, `GlDriverGapGuardTest` is what the cross-driver gap is measured with, and
re-authoring them on this device would spend that check. They passed unchanged, inside their 0.50 %
tolerance. `BACKLOG_v4_25.md` item 56 stays open and is now truer.

### 7.2 Broken on purpose, then reverted **[M]**

Two, one per backend, because two different things had to be shown to be checkable:

| what was broken | what caught it |
|---|---|
| one skin mask's weight, a 15×15 patch, by 12 levels | `PeopleLayerAssetTest`: *person_man_summer_walk0: 11* |
| the masks laid **over** instead of summed | `PeopleGoldenTest`: `people-skyscraper` 3.85 % of its focus against a 2.00 % limit, and `people-single` 0.21 % against 0.14 % |
| a sprite table referenced only from a doc comment | `SpriteReachabilityTest`: *PeopleLayerTable.kt: deadTableProbe* |
| the crossing folded **out** of the seed, i.e. the frozen deal back | `PeopleColoursTest`: *the three agree on 0.000 of crossings, where chance over three tones gives 1/9*, and *every outfit must be reachable* — only three of five ever were |

Every one reverted and re-run green. The fourth is worth its own sentence: it does not just fail,
it **reproduces the reported defect**. With the deal frozen, one theme's family of three comes out
three different tones and stays that way for ever, and two of the five outfits are never worn by
anybody. That is what the maintainer saw on `city`, and it is what `PeopleColoursTest` now stands
between.

### 7.3 What the full instrumented suite found that nothing else could **[M]**

Two failures on the first whole-suite run, both real and both fixed:

- **`VehicleScalePixelTest.CHILD_UNITS` is a fifth place the child's height lives.** The brief named
  four and all four moved together; this one expresses the same quantity in the **sprite's own
  units** rather than on the 80-unit metre, so it neither reads as the same number nor shares a
  spelling with the others — and it is only checkable by rendering a figure and measuring pixels.
  62 → 64 in v4.25, **54** now. `BACKLOG_v4_30.md` item 102.
- **`VehicleOccupantScaleTest`'s occupant palette named the wrong colour for the seatbelt.** Its
  seventh entry was `#EFDFC4`, labelled "the seatbelt (rc4 frontal busts)"; the seatbelt is drawn
  `#3A3F4A` and always has been — `#EFDFC4` is the man's summer trousers. That is the **third**
  wrong entry this one list has been caught with: v4.25 re-measured it and found two others, and
  this one survived that pass because a colour that matches nothing is invisible to a scan rather
  than noisy. The list is derived from `PeopleColours` now, and the assertion that would have
  caught it — *the seatbelt's colour must be painted where the seatbelt is drawn* — is the part
  that is new.

### 7.4 What drawing in layers costs the `Canvas` backend **[M]**

The GL backend pays six vertices per layer and no draw call (§4). The `Canvas` backend has no
batch, so it pays a blit. Measured on the device, same session, adjacent runs, a crowded `city`
frame at full density: **82.16 ms on v4.29 against 93.30 ms on v4.30 — +13.6 %.**

It lands only on the EGL fallback. The gallery and settings previews build a `CanvasSceneTarget`
too, but they draw a `ThemePreviewScene`, which blits the walkers' un-suffixed bases directly — one
blit each, unchanged. `BACKLOG_v4_30.md` item 103 has the numbers and what would remove it.

### 7.5 The release build, and an accidental proof **[M]**

`assembleRelease` builds clean — R8 and resource shrinking, which is the one place a table of 195
generated drawable names could go wrong silently. It did not, and the way it did not is worth
recording:

**Resource shrinking removed exactly 16 drawables, and they are exactly the 16 declared orphans** —
the eight window bases, the boy's six walk frames and the two girl `head_car` bases. Nothing else.
Read off the release APK's own resource table against the shipped directory:

```
$ aapt2 dump resources app-release-unsigned.apk | grep -oE 'drawable/[a-z0-9_]+' | sort -u
320 names, against 332 PNGs in res/drawable-nodpi
```

That is an independent check that **every drawable `PeopleLayerTable` names exists and is reached**:
a mask the table pointed at wrongly would have been shrunk away and the figure would have drawn
without it. And it says something the decoded ceiling's KDoc should know — the orphans it counts
*"bound the APK"* among other things, and these sixteen do not reach the APK at all.

### 7.6 The asset tooling's own suite **[M]**

109 tests, **two failing — and both were already failing on v4.29**, measured by extracting the base
ZIP into a scratch tree and running its own suite there. They are inherited, they are about lake
artwork rather than people, and they are recorded as `BACKLOG_v4_30.md` item 101 rather than made
green: raising `KNOWN_PENDING_CROP_COUNT` to match reality would record a drift as a decision nobody
took, and cropping the three sprites is an artwork change with a device look attached.

`paperscrape-assets probe` matches its recorded fingerprint, and `validate` reports 332 entries, 137
with an SVG source, 101 variant groups, all distinct.
