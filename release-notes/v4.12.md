# PaperScrape v4.12

One new thing you can turn on, and an answer to a question about how the buildings look.

## Let the app work out the other half of a colour

Every colour in the app that has a daytime and a night-time version — houses, buildings, cars,
umbrellas, people, trees, the seasonal decorations, both mountain layers, the hills, the lake, the
clouds, the sky and both kinds of precipitation — now has a small three-way switch under it:

- **Both** — what the app has always done. You pick the day colour and the night colour separately.
  This stays the default everywhere, and nothing about your existing themes changes.
- **Day sets night** — you pick the daytime colour and the night one follows it.
- **Night sets day** — the other way round.

Pick a bright red for daytime with "Day sets night" on, and the night version becomes a deeper,
calmer red on its own. The rule is the same for every object in the scene, and it is not a guess:
it was measured from the day/night pairs this app's own themes already use by hand, which turn out
to keep the hue exactly and simply take the lightness and the saturation down.

**You can always change your mind.** Switching a colour back to "Both" gives you back the two
colours you had picked yourself — they are never overwritten, only set aside while the switch is
on. The half the app is working out for you is shown greyed next to the one you own, so you can
see what the scene will actually use.

Saved themes, backups and theme files carry the setting with them. Anything saved before this
release opens exactly as it did.

## The skyscrapers have proper windows now

The tall buildings' windows used to be painted in whatever colour you had picked for the building
itself — a window the same shade as the bricks around it, which nothing else in the scene does.
Houses and the restaurant have always shown cool glass in daylight and warm light after dark.

The towers now do the same thing, through the same mechanism rather than one of their own: cool
blue-grey by day, warm yellow at night, crossfading with the rest of the scene as the light
changes. It works over any facade colour you pick, including the automatic day/night colours above.

## And the sharpness question, answered

The window grid was also reported as looking soft when a strong colour is used. It was measured
rather than guessed, and the result is the opposite of how it looked: with a saturated colour the
windows stand out *more* than they do with the Big City theme's own default grey-blue. The one case
where the grid fades is a very dark colour, where the whole building is meant to read as a dark mass
anyway. Nothing was changed for that, because nothing was wrong — and the change above is a separate
matter, about what colour a window is rather than how far it stands out.
