# v2.15

**Bad weather now looks like bad weather. Thunderstorms flash only when it is actually raining, and
snow has been watched falling from a real forecast.**

**The sky reacts to the forecast.** Until now, Live Weather could put clouds in the sky and rain in
the air, but the sky itself stayed the colour your theme painted it — so a thunderstorm could fall
out of a bright blue afternoon with the sun shining through it. Now the weather dims the whole
scene: the sky loses a little light and colour, the clouds go darker still, and the sun fades
behind them. It builds up gradually — an overcast day barely changes, light rain is a little
duller, heavy rain is grey and flat, a thunderstorm is the darkest of all.

**Your theme still looks like your theme.** This is a wash over the colours you chose, not a
different palette. A warm sunset stays warm as it darkens; two themes in the same storm still look
like two different themes.

**Day and night are untouched.** A storm never turns afternoon into night. The sun stays where it
belongs in the sky and keeps its arc — it just gets dimmer. At night the scene stays a night scene,
with the storm layered over it.

**Lightning comes out of the clouds now.** Bolts used to appear near the top of the sky, above the
clouds, and were far too big. They now strike from inside the cloud band and are about a third of
the size. How often they flash has not changed.

**Lightning is tied to the weather properly.** PaperScrape has always been able to draw a lightning
flash, and Live Weather already switched it on for a thunderstorm — but it did not check that
anything was falling first. Weather services publish a short summary code that can say
"thunderstorm" while every measurement of rain reads zero, and in that case the sky used to flash
over a dry scene. Now a storm needs both: the forecast calling it a thunderstorm *and* rain
actually coming down. Flashes stay what they were — occasional, a few seconds apart, never
constant.

**Snow, checked against real snowfall.** The snow path had only ever been tested against saved
responses. It has now been watched running against a live reading from a place that was genuinely
snowing, and it behaves the way it should: snow falls through the air, and that is all it does.

**Your theme decides whether the town looks wintry; the forecast decides what falls out of the
sky.** Real snow does not put snow on the roofs, cap the trees or put people in coats — that is the
winter look, which you turn on yourself for any theme you like. Pick the Winter theme while it is
snowing outside and you get both, from two independent settings. Christmas stays separate from
both, as it already did.

Nothing else changed.
