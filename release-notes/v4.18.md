# PaperScrape v4.18

The art-direction release. Seven local passes went into it, each judged frame by frame on a real
phone, and the street you see is the result: the traffic, the two shops and the night were all
drawn again from the question "what should this look like", not from what it happened to look like.

## A berlina that is a car

The saloon every road car is built from was redrawn from a blank sheet: a long bonnet, a cabin set
behind the midpoint, a real tail deck, a chrome side spear from nose to tail on the beltline, and
bigger wheels seated in matching arch cuts. The glasshouse is sized for its people — grown in the
final pass so a table-sized head sits in it with air to spare — and the beltline and spear sit
where the glass ends. Its ground shadow finally lies on the road instead of hovering at the
beltline, it carries a headlight and a red tail lamp, and it stands a touch taller — grown through
the size table, so nothing else in the scene moved.

**The police car** was refitted, not repainted: the horizontal livery rides the doors exactly to
the rocker and the light bar sits centred on the roof. **The taxi** finally has its roof sign — the
one thing that tells a yellow car from a yellow car — and a chequer band cut to the doors.

## A fire engine that is a fire engine

Aluminium roll-up shutters with real slats in every equipment bay, a stepped cab, a full-length
cream band, front bumper and rear tailboard, and a twin rear axle — the silhouette cue that says
*heavy vehicle* and nothing else. It reads from the far lane.

## Two shops with two trades

The restaurant is a trattoria: cream fascia with the fork-and-knife badge, full-width scalloped
awning over glass and door, framed wood-and-glass entrance, planters on the pavement — crowned by a
bow pediment, the only curved roofline in the village. The bar is a pub: painted deep-green joinery
front that darkens honestly into the night, cream fascia with a mug badge, mullioned shop windows,
a carriage lantern on the corner — crowned by raised corner piers with the name plate between them.
Cover every sign and the skyline still tells you which building is which. Winter lays its drifts on
the new crowns.

## The traffic knows it is night

Headlights and tail lamps come up on the same ramp the house windows use; the police beacon, the
taxi sign and the fire engine's lights come with them. By day the lamps rest unlit in their own
glass — amber ahead, red behind — and everything else stays dark until dusk.

## People you can actually see in the cars

The people in the vehicles are the same people who walk the pavement. One face language runs
through the whole scene now: the people behind the glass are frontal busts with the walkers' own
dot eyes and hair, a seatbelt across the chest saying "person in a car", in the same families,
seasons and skin tones the pedestrians rotate through. They are sized off the same height table
as every pedestrian — a driver's face measures a pedestrian's at the same depth, not a share of
whatever glass happened to be behind it — and **every car carries two of them**: a driver up
front where the wheel is, and a passenger behind, with daylight between their heads and a seat
back rising between their shoulders. Making room for them is why the saloon's cabin is longer and
its windscreen more upright than it was, and why the side window now follows the roof line
instead of sitting square inside it; the car itself is exactly the size it always was. They are
never the same person twice — the two seats always hold different people — and both sit clear of
the pillars, with air above every winter hat and not one pixel outside the glass. Since the
person no longer tells you which way the car is going, the lamps do: amber glass at the nose,
brake red at the tail, on every car, day and night. The fire engine keeps its single crew
member, its grown cab window and its real tandem axle. People at house and shop windows are
exactly as they were.

## Autumn survives the swipe

Swiping between home pages used to visibly rebuild the falling leaves: every leaf was re-dealt to
a different tree whenever the parallax carried a crown across a screen edge. Each tree now owns
its leaves outright — swipe away and back, and the same leaf is still mid-fall from the same
crown, right where the clock says it should be. The leaves also detach where leaves do — at the
canopy's lower edge, never painted on the foliage — and a big near crown sheds a canopy's worth
while a distant one sheds a few, so the effect neither collapses nor floods as the scene scrolls.

## The street presents itself

There is one trattoria and one pub in every scene now — no more meeting the same storefront
twice in one screen — and each claims a clear stretch of street on every theme: nothing parks in
front of a shop past forty percent of its whole face, canopy included, and no tree trunk or
umbrella pole ever stands across a doorway or a window. The commercial slots that used to crowd
the street as extra shop copies stand on the skyline as towers instead. The tower's entrance
light is an awning over its own door now, sitting on the ground inside the building's walls,
instead of a glowing shelf wider than the tower floating in front of it.

## And the ground got busier in the right ways

Snow piles and leaf piles keep their sliders, pumpkins their Halloween faces, umbrellas their spot
beside the doors, and falling leaves their own tree's foot — everything from the passes that led
here, now in one release.
