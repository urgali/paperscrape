# v4.1 — the people system

`versionCode = 32`, `versionName = "4.1"`. Baseline: v4.0 (`versionCode = 31`).

One subject only: the figures walking the pavement and standing at the windows.
Cars, roads, traffic and window *rendering* are untouched, and the regression
evidence for that is at the bottom of this note.

---

## Why

A user reported four things from two screenshots of a running device:

1. when two pedestrians overlap, the farther one is drawn on top of the nearer one;
2. one direction of travel is always a man and a boy, the other always a woman and a girl;
3. the skin tones look tied to the direction of travel;
4. the density setting seems to make no difference between 20% and 100%.

All four turned out to be **one defect**, and it was arithmetic rather than an
unlucky seed. Every attribute of a pedestrian was a function of its candidate
index, and the pool held exactly as many candidates as there were sprite kinds:

```kotlin
val reverse = i % 2 == 1              // direction
val near    = i % 2 == 0              // pavement row
val kindIdx = i % personKinds.size    // which of man/woman/boy/girl
```

With `PEDESTRIAN_COUNT == 4` and four kinds, the periods 2 and 4 lock together:

| i | kind  | row  | direction |
|---|-------|------|-----------|
| 0 | man   | near | right     |
| 1 | woman | far  | left      |
| 2 | boy   | near | right     |
| 3 | girl  | far  | left      |

So the composition *was* the direction, on every device and in every theme. The
"skin tone follows the direction" report is this same table seen through the
artwork, since each sprite carries its own baked palette. And the draw order was
the index order, so candidate 1 — on the **far** row — was drawn after candidate
0 on the near row and covered it.

---

## What changed

**Groups, not candidates.** The density pool still holds four slots, so the
slider keeps mapping onto the same five outcomes it always did, but a surviving
slot now yields a group of **one to three** people. Every attribute is read from
its own channel of the existing `CandidateNoise`: group size, direction and row
per group; age, sex and skin per member. Because that noise is *addressed*
rather than *consumed*, no attribute can perturb another.

**Depth comes from the baseline.** The population is returned sorted far-to-near
and drawn in that order, so the nearest figure lands on top. The sort sees a flat
list and consults the group only as a tie-break, so ordering inside a group of
three is correct for the same reason ordering between groups is.

**Density's real meaning, stated.** It is the fraction of the group pool that is
present — not opacity, not scale, not speed. It was always applied; what made it
look inert is that four candidates admit only five outcomes, and every density
from 1% to 35% selected the same single pedestrian. Groups widen the range
without changing the parameter's meaning.

**A latent bug fixed inside the people system.**
`CandidateThreshold.offsetFor(PEDESTRIAN_THRESHOLD_SALT)` was being passed
`6151` where it expects an `EffectId` ordinal, and returned `683.5`. It survived
only because the thresholds take a fractional part — and `frac(683.5)` is exactly
`0.5`, which is `MOUNTAINS_BACK`'s offset. The salt whose stated job was to
decorrelate pedestrians from every other category had pinned them to one. Fixed
with the people system's own constant rather than by adding an ordinal, because
`offsetFor` divides by `EffectId.COUNT` and raising it would have moved clouds,
birds, sailboats and dolphins in every theme.

**People at more windows.** v4.0 populated two windows in the whole game: one
pane of the small house and one of the large house's four. Now every house
window is a candidate, and commercial frontage and skyscrapers are too, at rates
chosen per building kind so a tower does not become a wall of faces.

**Window occupants are chosen properly.** v4.0 read presence (`% 3`) and identity
(`% 4`) from one truncated float with no channel separation, so who appeared was
a function of whether anyone appeared. The two questions are now separate
channels of the shared noise, addressed by building and window.

## Skin tone

**Three tones, as real artwork, chosen automatically.**

Every character now exists in three skin tones: 96 variant PNGs generated by
`tools/generate_skin_variants.py`. The art is flat — each character's skin is a
single colour with no shading ramp — so a variant moves that one colour and
nothing else. Interior skin pixels are replaced outright; anti-aliased edge
pixels are decomposed into the blend they actually are and shifted by their own
skin fraction, so silhouettes and outlines survive to the byte. Where a tone
equals a character's original colour the variant is byte-identical to its source.

The generator verifies itself: every non-skin colour must keep exactly the pixel
mask it had, or the variant is rejected. `SkinToneAssetsTest` re-derives that
same claim from the shipped PNGs rather than trusting the script, so "clothes,
hair, eyes and outlines are untouched" is a checked property.

All three tones are shipped PaperScrape paint already — the woman's, the man's
and the boy's own skin colours — so the palette is the artwork's own and cannot
drift out of style.

**A fourth, deeper tone was made and dropped.** At the depth needed to read as a
distinct tone it converged on the woman's brown hair and on the shared outline,
and a face whose skin matches its own hair stops reading as a face; lightening it
far enough to separate put it on top of the boy's brown. A fourth tone needs an
art pass on hair and outlines, not another recolour.

**Skin is not a setting, and deliberately not one.** No preference, slider,
DataStore key or UI control selects a tone. The only input is the `CH_SKIN`
channel on the seed, read independently of direction, age, sex, group size,
position in the group, building kind and window index. `SkinToneAssetsTest`
fails if a skin preference ever appears in the sources.

**What it costs.** A variant is the same canvas as its source, so the sprite set
grows from 14.79 MB to 25.67 MB decoded and `SpriteGeometryTest`'s budget was
raised from 16 MB to 26 MB. That test says plainly that raising the budget is a
memory decision rather than a test fix, and it is recorded as one: a live
wallpaper runs in a process the system kills freely, and this doubles the worst
case that sizing assumes. It is a ceiling rather than a resident figure — sprites
decode on demand and no frame draws three tones of one character — but the
ceiling moved. Recolouring into a cached bitmap at load would give the same tones
with no growth here; it was not taken because this batch asked for real variant
PNGs, and it remains the cheaper answer if device memory pressure is ever
measured to be a problem.

## Regression

`AUTO = INVARIATO.` No file under the vehicle, road or traffic path was opened.
Pedestrian sorting is a `sortWith` on a list the people system builds and owns;
the car draw loop was not touched, and no shared sorting utility was introduced
that could reach it.

`WINDOW RENDERING = INVARIATO.` No window sprite, size, position, colour, lit
state or draw call changed. `drawWindowOccupant` stands a bust at a sill and has
no opinion about the window; the skyscraper's panes are painted into
`skyscraper_wall` and are not redrawn.

All 875 tests that existed at v4.0 still pass, unmodified.
