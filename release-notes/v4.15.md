# PaperScrape v4.15

A closing pass. The people in cars fit their windows, the people at windows stop dressing for weather
they are not in, the tower's height is the height it draws, and a long list of smaller things that
had been carried from one release to the next is finished.

## People in cars fit the car

With a winter theme, a passenger's bobble hat stood above the side window and was painted onto the
roof, and the driver's beanie clipped the top edge. The heads were the right size; the window was
sized against one sprite of a family whose members are not all the same height.

**The window grew rather than the people shrinking.** Shrinking them was tried and measured first,
and it re-opened the problem the scale exists to prevent: people in cars started reading smaller than
the people walking past. The side glass is now about 9% taller, the door livery on the police car and
the taxi follows the sill down with it, and everybody is inside the glass. A passenger's head is
exactly the size it has always been; a driver's is very slightly larger.

## People at windows are indoors

Someone leaning out of their own kitchen window was putting on a woolly hat because it had started
snowing outside their house. Window occupants now keep their indoor look in every season. Pedestrians
and people in cars are unchanged — a car is a coat, not a house.

## The tower is the height it says

The office block declared 16.8 m and drew 15.6, because the number had been measured to the tip of
its aerial while every other building measures its walls. The declaration was corrected to match the
drawing. **Nothing on screen moved** — the correction was to the bookkeeping, not to the skyline.

## Other fixes

- A backup or theme file containing "NaN" is refused instead of quietly poisoning a coordinate that
  then survives restarts.
- Importing a file no longer reads it without limit, so picking the wrong file from a downloads
  folder cannot exhaust memory. The same bound now applies to the three network responses.
- If the saved-themes file ever becomes unreadable, the next setting you change no longer overwrites
  it. Your themes stay on disk.
- Backups record which theme format they hold, so a future version can read an old one correctly.
- An update download is now checked against the signature of the copy on your phone before you are
  offered the install prompt, not only against a checksum published beside it.
- Changing a setting at the exact moment the screen rotates no longer loses the change.
- A low-memory hint no longer throws away every uploaded texture and immediately re-uploads it.
- With the settings screen open beside the wallpaper preview, one change is now one redraw.
- Pedestrians no longer stand with their feet on the road.
- Weather checks stop entirely while the wallpaper cannot be seen, and catch up the moment it can.
  Nothing is missed, and there is no longer a timer ticking behind a screen that is off.

## Two more that had been carried between releases

**An update download no longer restarts when the screen rotates.** Rotating the phone, switching
light and dark, or changing the font size used to tear the settings screen down and take the transfer
with it. It doesn't any more. Genuinely leaving the screen still cancels the download, which is what
backing out of an update should do.

**An interrupted restore no longer leaves things half-changed.** Restoring a backup writes your
settings and your saved themes to two separate places, and if the app was killed between the two —
by the system reclaiming memory, or a battery pulled — you could end up with the new settings and
the old themes. Now the restore finishes itself the next time the app or the wallpaper starts, so a
restore either happened or it didn't.

## Under the hood

The comparison used for the OpenGL screenshot tests was measuring how much outline a scene contains
rather than whether the scene changed, so the same correct frame failed on one phone and passed on
another. It now compares the flat interiors of shapes and, separately, whether any outline has
actually moved — strict on both, and no longer confused by two graphics drivers rounding an edge
differently. The whole test suite passes on both test devices for the first time.
