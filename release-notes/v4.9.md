# PaperScrape v4.9

Live Weather now says the same thing on the screen and in the sky, and recovers from a dropped
request in minutes instead of an hour.

## A dropped request cost an hour

The refresh timer was stamped when a fetch was *started*, not when it succeeded, and nothing reset
it when one failed. So a single request lost to a flaky connection left the wallpaper on the wrong
weather until the next hourly refresh — the loop checked thirty times in between and refused every
one. The most common way to hit it was simply switching the phone on: the wallpaper starts before
the network is up, and that first failed fetch cost the following hour.

A failure that a retry can actually fix — no connection, a timeout, a garbled reply — is now retried
after 2 minutes, then 4, 8, 16, 32, and from there at the normal hourly rate. That is four extra
requests during an outage and none once it has settled. A rejected key, a missing key or an exceeded
quota are answers rather than accidents, so they keep the normal schedule: asking again sooner would
not change what comes back.

## The screen and the sky could disagree

Turning the location off, or switching to a weather service whose key is missing or rejected, put
Live Weather into a state the settings screen describes as *"the scene is running on this theme's own
weather"* — and unlocks that theme's cloud and precipitation controls so you can set them. But the
last fetched conditions kept drawing. The controls you had just been handed did nothing, and the sky
contradicted the screen describing it.

Whether real conditions may draw the scene is now one decision, made in one place, and both the
settings screen and the wallpaper read that same answer. When the screen says the theme is driving
the scene, the theme is driving the scene.

## Conditions no longer go stale forever

Keeping the last known-good weather through a dropped request is deliberate — it stops the scene
flicking back to the theme's own settings for a single tick. But there was no end to it: a phone
that had been offline since yesterday carried on drawing yesterday's thunderstorm.

Fetched conditions now expire after three hours. Past that the scene returns to the theme's own
weather, which is always a valid scene, and the status says so. Three hours is long enough that a
late refresh, a phone in a pocket or one dropped request never causes a flicker, and short enough
that the sky on screen is one you could still walk outside and recognise.

## Nothing else changed

No rendering code was touched, and no scene, sprite or colour is drawn differently than in v4.8. The
four location modes — Off, GPS, Network and Custom — keep exactly the meanings they had, no setting
was renamed or moved, and backups made with earlier versions import unchanged.

The scheduling rules were also pulled out of the wallpaper service into one place that tests can
reach, with 23 new tests covering retry timing, expiry and the agreement between screen and sky.
They use fixed timestamps rather than a real clock, so they check the behaviour rather than the
weather of the day they happen to run on.
