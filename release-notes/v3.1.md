# v3.1

**A corrupted settings file can no longer take the wallpaper down with it, Live Weather always has a
way out, and a leaping dolphin stops flying through sails.**

A small, deliberately narrow release: five fixes found by a full review of v3.0, and nothing else.
No new features, no changes to how anything works when it is working.

---

## Your wallpaper survives a damaged settings file

**This is the one that mattered.** PaperScrape keeps your settings, your saved themes and your
"remind me later" choice in three small files. If one of them was damaged — a battery pulled at the
wrong moment, storage full, a write interrupted — the app crashed the instant it tried to read it.
And because the settings live in the same process that draws the wallpaper, that crash took the
wallpaper with it: Android replaced PaperScrape with its own plain background, and it happened again
every time the app tried to start. The only way out was to clear the app's data, which threw away
*everything* — including your custom themes, which had nothing to do with the damaged file.

Now a damaged file is simply started over. The app opens, the wallpaper keeps drawing, and only the
settings from the file that was actually damaged go back to their defaults. Everything in the other
two files is left exactly as it was.

The three cases are also kept apart properly: a file that cannot be read *at all* is replaced once
and for good, while a storage hiccup that might clear up on its own is waited out rather than being
treated as a reason to throw your settings away.

Checked by deliberately damaging each of the three files in turn, restarting, and rebooting the
phone: no crash, the wallpaper still running, the damaged file back at its defaults, the other two
untouched byte for byte.

## Live Weather can always be switched off

There was a corner you could get stuck in. Turn Live Weather on, then set Location back to Off — or
turn off "Follow real time" — and the Live Weather switch went grey while still reading *on*. The
weather controls under World & scene were locked "because Live Weather is on", every message on
screen told you to go and turn it off, and the switch that would have done it no longer responded.

**A setting that is on can now always be turned off.** What the prerequisites decide is whether it
can be turned *on*, which is what they were always meant to decide.

The two screens also stopped contradicting each other. World & scene used to label clouds and rain
"Driven by Live Weather" and lock them whenever the switch was on — including when there was no
location and the forecast was not driving anything, while Weather & time's own banner was
simultaneously admitting exactly that. Those controls now go read-only only while real conditions
are genuinely in charge of the scene, and stay yours the rest of the time.

## Dolphins go behind sails

With a busy lake, a dolphin at the top of its leap could be drawn straight across the sail of a boat
further out. It did not read as one thing passing in front of another: it read as a dolphin flying
through the rigging.

The cause was that everything on the water was sorted by where it *sits*, while a sail stands about
four lane-widths above its own waterline. So a dolphin a single lane nearer than a boat — correctly
painted after it — ended up over the sail.

A leaping dolphin is now sorted by where its body actually is, so it slides behind the boat whose
waterline it has climbed above and comes back in front as it lands. Nothing else about the lake
moved: same boats, same speeds, same paths, same lanes, and two overlapping boats still read exactly
as they did in v3.0.

## "Check for updates" tells the truth when it cannot check

Pressing **Check for updates** with no connection used to answer **"You're up to date"**. It had not
checked anything. It now says it could not check, and says why — no connection, GitHub not
answering, or a reply it could not read.

The automatic check at launch is unchanged and still stays quiet when it fails: a question nobody
asked does not deserve an error message.

## Coordinates read the same everywhere

On an Italian, French or German phone a coordinate pair came out as `45,464, 9,190` — the same comma
separating the two numbers and sitting inside each of them, which is not readable as a coordinate by
anyone. Coordinates are now always written `45.464, 9.190`, wherever you are.

Numbers that *should* follow your language still do: a speed multiplier on an Italian phone still
reads `1,5x`.
