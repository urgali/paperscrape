# v2.14

**The sky matches the forecast again, the bottom of every settings screen is reachable, and Live
Weather can use a second service.**

**Rain no longer falls on a dry afternoon.** Open-Meteo publishes a short summary code alongside its
actual measurements, and the two can disagree — the code was reading "slight rain showers" for
Florence while every measurement of rain, showers, snow and total precipitation read zero, and the
wallpaper believed the code. It now believes the measurements: if the forecast says nothing is
falling, nothing falls. The reverse still holds too, which is what v2.13 fixed — if millimetres are
measurably falling while the summary still says "overcast", it rains.

**Clouds and rain agree with each other.** If you had switched the cloud layer off in *World &
scene*, Live Weather used to draw its rain and skip its clouds, so a storm arrived as rain out of an
empty sky. While Live Weather is on, the forecast now drives clouds exactly as completely as it
already drove rain — including drawing none at all when the forecast reports a clear sky.

**Scroll to the end of a settings screen and the last row is all there.** It was cut off before —
a switch half under the gesture bar, a button with its bottom edge missing, a line of text
truncated mid-sentence. This had been "fixed" twice by adding space at the bottom, and it kept
coming back, because the space was never the problem: the screens are full-screen dialogs, and
their content was being laid out taller than the window that shows it, so the last couple of
centimetres were outside the window entirely and no amount of padding could bring them back. Each
screen is now sized to its own window. Checked on a phone, scrolled all the way down, on every
settings screen and with the keyboard both open and closed.

**A second weather provider.** Live Weather now offers a choice under *Weather & time → Weather
provider*: **Open-Meteo** (the default, unchanged, and needs no account) or **Visual Crossing**.
Switching between them keeps your location and every other weather setting, and each remembers its
own API key.

Visual Crossing needs a key — it has no free anonymous tier the way Open-Meteo does. A free account
gives 1,000 weather records a day, far more than one hourly refresh uses. Until you enter one the
app tells you so and makes no requests at all, and Open-Meteo is one tap away.

**If the provider you picked can't be reached, it stays picked.** The app says what happened and
keeps showing the last conditions it managed to fetch; it does not quietly ask the other service
instead.

**Phone location stopped fetching the wrong town's weather.** If you had a custom location set and
then switched to *Phone*, Live Weather carried on using the custom coordinates. It now notices the
change.

A caveat worth repeating: live weather refreshes at most once an hour, so a shower that starts just
after a refresh can take up to an hour to reach the wallpaper. Turning Live Weather on, changing
provider, or entering a key all fetch straight away rather than waiting for that hour.
