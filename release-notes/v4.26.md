# v4.26

- **The sea is a mirror.** The water used to be a flat rectangle with six pale stripes and four
  lines drawn across it. It now reflects the sky it sits under — a gradient from the sky's own
  colour at the far edge into your lake colour at the near one — with the sun's or the moon's light
  laid across it as a path of cut glints that widens as it comes toward you. Three ways of drawing
  water were tried on a real phone: a swell and rows of brush strokes both read as stripes at the
  height this band actually is, and the mirror read as water.
- **The far edge of the water is a cut edge.** Because the water now reflects the sky, some themes
  brought the two colours close enough that the shoreline started to disappear. There is a struck
  line along it now, and it only appears where it is needed and only as strongly as it needs to —
  in a theme where the sky and the water are already different, you will not see it at all.
- **The clouds are soft.** They were a cut-paper shape with a hard edge and a second sheet behind
  them. A cloud is not a piece of paper standing in the scene, it is weather, so it is now one soft
  mass with a feathered edge and a single tone falling from white at the crown to grey at the base.
- **The birds are birds.** They were drawn nearly twice the size a bird should be at that distance,
  as a flat angular shape whose wing-beat did nothing visible. They are now about a third smaller —
  a little taller than a person, which is what a bird looks like from across a valley — with a
  plump body, a fan tail and two round wings that actually beat.
- **The dolphins can be seen at night.** They were painted a blue-grey almost exactly the colour of
  a night sea, so on the darker themes and in a storm they simply were not there. They are a light
  grey now, chosen by measuring the animal against every water colour the twelve built-in themes
  can produce rather than by eye, and they read on all of them.
- **The boats and the dolphins are drawn in the scene's own language** — layered paper with the
  sheet below showing at the edge, the same way the trees, the sky and the people were redrawn in
  the last three releases. The lake is now the last part of the scene to have been left behind.
- **The water costs less to draw than it did.** Roughly half the drawing work per frame, including
  no longer painting a copy of the sea that sits entirely off the side of your screen.

**Nothing you have set changes.** No setting was added, removed or renumbered, no saved theme is
touched, and every scene you have built looks like itself — with a better sky and a better sea in
it.
