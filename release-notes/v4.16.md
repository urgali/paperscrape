# PaperScrape v4.16

The people in the cars are the right size now. They were being drawn to a different rule from
everybody else at a window, and the rule the rest of the scene uses turned out to be the one that
looks right.

## Heads in car windows

A person seen through a house, shop or tower window has been drawn the same way since v4.2: the head
takes about half the pane and the rest stays glass. A person in a car was drawn to a different rule
— the bust was made exactly as tall as the window — so the head filled nearly three quarters of it
and the top of the head touched the roof line of the window, on every car, in every season, at every
distance. That is why they read as too big for the car.

Car occupants now follow the same rule as everyone else at a window. A head takes 51.9% of the pane
it is seen through, whether that pane is a bedroom window or a windscreen, and there is a fifth to a
quarter of the pane as clear glass above every head.

Drivers, passengers and the fire engine's crew all obey it, so nobody in the scene is drawn to a
size of their own any more.

## What did not change

The cars are the same size. So is the road, the carriageway, the lanes, the window, the sill and the
livery on the doors — the police stripe and the taxi chequer are exactly where they were. No
artwork changed. Both a bigger car and a bigger window were built and looked at on a phone first:
neither one changes how big a person looks *inside* the car, because an occupant is drawn in the
car's own coordinates and grows with it. A bigger car only crowds the road.

Winter hats, hoods and scarves still belong to the people outside — pedestrians and the people in
cars — and the people at house and office windows still stay in their indoor clothes whatever the
season. That rule is unchanged and was checked again, in winter and at Christmas, on the phone.

## Under the bonnet

The comparison that used to guard the occupants' size measured heads in scene metres, which is the
one quantity the perspective then cancels out. Two heads the same size in metres are drawn
differently when one stands on the road and the other on the pavement behind it, so the check was
measuring depth rather than proportion. It has been replaced by the things that actually matter: how
much of its own window an occupant fills, how big they are against their own vehicle, and that an
occupant is drawn at exactly their vehicle's distance and nothing else.

The graphics comparison that keeps the scene stable across different phones now also catches a scene
drawn at the wrong scale and a scene whose pieces have been rearranged, on top of the five kinds of
damage it already caught.
