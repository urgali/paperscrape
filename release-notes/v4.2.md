# v4.2 — the street the seeds actually produce

`versionCode = 33`, `versionName = "4.2"`. Baseline: v4.1 (`versionCode = 32`), the
published tag, not a local checkpoint.

Two runtime defects, both reported from a phone running v4.1, and nothing else.
Cars, roads, traffic, weather, location, the updater, `targetSdk`, CI and the UI
are untouched.

---

## Why

v4.1 replaced the people system and proved its claims with 55 new tests, all of
which passed. The build then went on a phone, and:

- the pedestrians were **almost always girls**, almost always on the darkest of
  the three skin tones; adult men were nearly absent, adult women nearly absent,
  and a boy was seen once;
- **nobody was ever behind the glass of a bar, a restaurant or any other
  non-residential building**, though v4.1 reported "commercial = 3/3 populatable
  panes".

Both reports were right, and both are about the gap between *reachable* and
*produced*.

---

## Defect 1 — a frozen sample, not an unreachable value

v4.1 chose every attribute of a person by comparing one hashed value against a
constant:

```kotlin
val age  = if (CandidateNoise.value(seed, addr, CH_AGE) < 0.5f) ADULT else CHILD
val sex  = if (CandidateNoise.value(seed, addr, CH_SEX) < 0.5f) MALE  else FEMALE
val skin = (CandidateNoise.value(seed, addr, CH_SKIN) * 3).toInt()
```

That is a fair coin. Over hundreds of synthetic seeds it produces a perfect
distribution, which is exactly what v4.1's tests measured.

**A wallpaper never draws hundreds of seeds. It draws one.** The seed is
`themeId.hashCode()`, it never changes, and the street it makes is at most twelve
people. A fair coin flipped six times clumps — and because the seed is frozen,
the clump is frozen with it.

Measured on the twelve shipped theme ids, at each theme's own default density,
v4.1's `beach` street was:

```
girl/skin2  girl/skin2  girl/skin2  boy/skin2  girl/skin2  girl/skin2
```

Six people. Five girls. Every one on tone 2. No adult of either sex. On every
device, in every session, permanently. `beach` is the theme the report came from,
and it matches the report line for line.

It was not only `beach`. **Ten of the twelve built-in themes** were missing at
least one of {adult male, adult female, boy, girl, a skin tone, a direction of
travel}: `tundra` had all ten of its people walking rightward, `sunset` and
`new_year` had no girl at all, `winter` no adult male, `desert` and `easter` no
adult female. Eight of the twelve never showed one of the three group sizes. And
across the whole catalogue **not one adult male ever walked alone**: of fifteen
lone walkers, three were women, five boys and seven girls.

### The fix

**Stratified selection.** The multiset of values dealt across a small pool is
fixed; the seed decides *which slot gets which*. A seeded permutation instead of
a seeded coin per slot.

This is not a new idea in this codebase, and deliberately not. `CandidateThreshold`
already argues the same case for density, in its own words: *"A hashed threshold
would be independent per candidate, which for a small pool clumps badly: with
four candidates at 50% you would often get one or three rather than two."*
Density got the low-discrepancy treatment in v3. The attributes did not. v4.2 is
that correction applied to the axis that was missed.

Concretely, in the new `SeededBalance`:

- the four groups are **ranked** on a channel rather than tested against a
  constant, and the ranks are handed values from a fixed multiset;
- the four person kinds `[man, woman, boy, girl]` are dealt whole across the four
  groups' *m*-th members, so every street carries an adult of each sex and a
  child of each sex however few people it has — and age and sex come out
  *exactly* independent, each 50/50 with a 25% joint, rather than approximately so;
- group sizes are dealt from `{1, 2, 3}`, so every street shows a lone walker, a
  pair and a trio;
- skin tones are dealt from `{0, 1, 2}` with a seeded rotation deciding which tone
  gets the spare fourth slot;
- direction and pavement row split the four groups two and two.

**The addresses are unchanged from v4.1.** A group is still addressed by its
index and a member by `groupIndex * MAX_GROUP_SIZE + memberIndex`; only the
decision made with the value has moved. Nothing counts the people already
produced, nothing forces "a male every N", and no attribute is read to choose
another — so the stability contract holds exactly as before: lowering the density
removes particular groups and leaves the rest untouched.

After the fix, **all twelve themes** carry an adult male, an adult female, a boy,
a girl, all three tones, both directions and groups of one, two and three, and no
kind or tone takes three quarters of any street.

## Defect 2 — the restaurant had no call site

v4.1's report said "commercial = 3/3 populatable panes". True, and about the
**bar**. The scene draws two street-level businesses, and the other one is the
**restaurant** — two to four buildings per theme against the bar's roughly one.
`drawRestaurantBuilding` had no `drawWindowOccupant` call at all.

On `beach`, `new_year` and `spring` the layout contains **no bar**. So on the
theme the defect was reported from, the number of commercial windows that could
hold a person was exactly **zero**, and four of the twelve themes had no
commercial occupant anywhere.

No test over `WindowOccupants` could have caught that: the object was never
asked. A missing call is only visible from outside the callee.

### The fix

The restaurant's frontage now takes occupants. Its window sprite is measured
rather than guessed: `restaurant_window` is 30x22 local units carrying **two
glass panes** at sprite pixels 8..39 and 50..81, so it gets two occupant slots,
one behind each pane, drawn at the same bust size a house's and the bar's use.
The window drawing itself is byte for byte what it was.

Occupancy also stopped being a coin per pane. `WindowOccupants` now **deals a
count** across a building's own windows — `floor(windowCount * rate + u)`, which
preserves each kind's declared rate exactly and removes the empty tail. Three
independent coins at 40% leave a bar with nobody 21.6% of the time; a dealt count
gives it one or two people and never none.

Houses and towers get the same treatment, because a rule that applied only to
shopfronts would be the special case this release exists to remove.

Every theme now has people behind commercial glass — between two and six of them.

## Found while measuring: the walk frame followed the sort order

Not reported, and not something a population test could see.

```kotlin
for ((personIndex, person) in population.withIndex()) {
    val frame = elapsedSeconds.frameIndex(3.2f, personIndex.toFloat(), 4)
```

`population` is sorted by depth, so **inserting one pedestrian renumbered every
pedestrian sorted behind it and stepped their legs to a different frame.** Moving
the People slider by one notch re-animated the survivors — precisely the
stability `CandidateThreshold` exists to provide, broken in the one place it was
not being checked. Found by a pixel measurement that reported 153 disturbed
pixels for a step whose only new figure was the farthest on the street, which
looked at first like a depth-order violation and was not.

The stagger now comes from the figure's own address. `PeopleOcclusionTest` is the
guard.

## Goldens: no longer `@Ignore`d

v4.1 wrote eight people-golden scenes and parked every assertion behind
`@Ignore`, honestly, because that session had no device. This one had an
emulator, so **all eight PNGs were rendered by the shipped drawing code, looked
at, and committed**, and the annotations are gone.

The sixteen pre-existing Canvas goldens were **stale**: v4.1 changed the people
and never re-took them, so thirteen of them were failing on an emulator the
moment one was available. They are regenerated here, and the regeneration is
justified mechanically rather than asserted — every differing pixel across all
sixteen lies between rows 509 and 654, inside the band the pedestrians and window
busts occupy. Nothing outside it moved.

`traffic-day` and `traffic-night` are the proof that the vehicles did not: their
diffs are pedestrians, and intersecting them with the pixel mask of everything
the vehicles paint leaves **1 pixel of 18 712** in one frame and **8 of 18 712**
in the other, all on a single row where a pedestrian's feet meet a car's roofline.

## Scope

Changed: `PedestrianPopulation`, `WindowOccupants`, the people paths of
`SceneObjectRenderer`, one new file (`SeededBalance`), tests, goldens, docs,
version.

Not changed: cars, roads, traffic, vehicle geometry, window rendering, weather,
location, the updater, `compileSdk`, `targetSdk`, CI, dependency versions, the
UI, and the skin-tone artwork.
