This release adds a button to update your location at once and says how old the position is, makes the menus lighter to read, brings shooting stars to the night sky, darkens the autumn trees at night, puts snow on the cars and gives the lying snow the colour of the ground, rebuilds the fireworks, and spreads the town below the road over several levels.

If you have 5.16 on Android 9 or 10: the update button in the app cannot install 5.21 there, because the fault is in the 5.16 that does the installing. Download 5.21 from the release page on GitHub and install it from there, once. From 5.17 on, updating from the app works on Android 9 and 10 too.

Your location

- With GPS or Network chosen and working, a new row Update location now sits under your position (or under No position from your phone yet) on the Weather & time page. Tap it and the app asks your phone where it is, for up to a minute, and says what happened: the position updated, or none found (indoors the GPS receiver often cannot see the satellites). With Live Weather on, the weather for the new place is asked as soon as you are back on the home screen.

- With Custom chosen and Live Weather on, a row Update weather now asks the weather for your place again as soon as you are back on the home screen, instead of at the next hourly check.

- The row of your position now says how old it is, for example "Resolved from the GPS receiver, 3 h ago". A position saved by 5.20 shows its age once a new one arrives.

- If your phone gives PaperScrape only your approximate location, choosing GPS now says so with a row GPS needs your precise location, and what to do: allow the precise location, or choose Network, which works with the approximate one.

- Choosing GPS or Network now starts one search instead of two. The lines under GPS and Network, and the one shown while no position has arrived yet, now say that the searches after one that found nothing are made while PaperScrape is on screen.

The menus

- A long line under a setting shows three lines and More; tap More to read it all. The coloured lines, which say what is on or what is missing, stay whole.
- On Seasons & decorations, each holiday names only what is on.
- The colour squares have a clearer edge, and the Both / Day sets night / Night sets day labels are larger.
- In the Themes gallery the three dots of a card sit on a small dark disc, so they show over a bright sky.
- A slider's value stays on one line.
- The pages slide in from the right.

The sky

- Shooting stars: on a night with stars a streak of light crosses the sky now and then, about once every three minutes, behind the clouds. They are on in every theme while Show Stars is on; switch them off with Shooting stars on the Stars page.

- The fireworks are rebuilt: each burst is one of four kinds at random (a paper flower, flying paper, sparks, sparks with light), never the same kind twice in a row, each with its rocket; now and then two more follow within a second, and every three minutes a finale of seven. The colours are picked at random among six, and some bursts mix two or three. The New Year's Eve card in the Themes gallery shows three paper flowers.

Autumn and winter

- With the Autumn palette, the trees' crowns darken at night as the falling leaves do, on the theme's card in the Themes gallery too.

- With the Winter palette (Seasons & decorations; Winter, Christmas, Tundra and New Year's Eve have it on), the cars carry snow on their roofs, the fire truck aside.

- With the Winter palette, the hills and the mountains are snow, and all the snow lying on roofs, trees, firs, drifts and cars takes the colour of the ground, a step lighter, by day and by night. On a theme whose ground is not snow, Winter's snow is used. While the palette is on, the Hills and Mountains pages show the snow's colours and they cannot be changed there; your own colours come back when you turn it off. The themes' cards in the gallery show the snow too. Falling snow, snowmen and frosted palms are unchanged.

Below the road

- The town below the road is no longer on one line: the farther a house, tree or building stands, the higher and smaller it is, and the flowers, leaves, pumpkins and drifts of that meadow are smaller near the road too. The same houses, trees and buildings as before, with more meadow free between them.

Building

- The tool that builds the app is up to date (Gradle 9.9.2, which fixes four security issues of the earlier version on the machine that builds); nothing in the app itself changes for it.

The work for your phone

- On our test phone the wallpaper uses about the same share of the processor as 5.20 on an autumn noon and night, a Christmas night with everything on, Big City at noon and a Halloween night in heavy rain (0.04 to 0.43 points of one core less). The new fireworks cost a little more: on New Year's Eve at 22:00 about one point of one core more (40.9 % instead of 39.9 %, 2.4 % more). The memory is about the same, and a frame comes a moment late about as often as before.

If you are updating from 5.20:

- Shooting stars are on wherever Show Stars is on: switch them off on the Stars page if you prefer.
- If your theme has the Autumn palette, its trees are darker at night; if it has the Winter palette, its hills, mountains and lying snow are in the snow's colours, and the cars carry snow. Your own colours for the hills and mountains are kept and come back with the palette off.
- The fireworks and the town below the road look as described above.
- The row of your position shows its age once your phone gives a new position.

Nothing else you have set has changed: your themes, colours and sliders are as you left them.

Still the case since v5.3: this version does not install on Intel-based devices or emulators, Chromebooks running Android apps included. v5.2 remains the last version that does.
